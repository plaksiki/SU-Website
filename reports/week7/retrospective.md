# Sprint 5 Retrospective

## What went well

1. On the backend, we completed core API functionality: Swagger/OpenAPI documentation, PUT endpoints for events and polls, and a separate export feature.

2. On the frontend, we finished admin-panel improvements: a dedicated section for admin panel features, plus photo optimization for events and departments.

3. The team stayed in touch every day via chat in Telegram, which allowed us to quickly share updates without waiting for formal meetings.

4. The team completed all planned sprint tasks on time. All assignments task were split between members and completed in time too.
   
## What did not go well

1. Not all team members had a deep enough understanding of the project to write relevant, accurate documentation.

2. The meeting with the customer happened too late in the Sprint, which forced the team to rework several things in a rush afterward

3. We could not find the perfect time for the meeting so one of the customers and one member of the development team were not able to participate

## What the team changed or attempted to change based on the previous Sprint Retrospective, and what results they observed

Based on the previous retrospective, we shifted our workflow and team interaction approach. Two frontend developers focused more on improving existing features and fixing bugs on the frontend side, as well as visual polish, which allowed the third member to concentrate on more complex tasks — backend-frontend integration and implementing new features. We also changed our approach to handling conflicts, addressing disagreements directly and early instead of letting them build up. As a result, we redesigned the admin panel based on the customer's revisions and finalized the documentation, and the team became more cohesive.

## Action points

Sprint 5 was the final Sprint of the course, so these action points are framed as recommendations for continued maintenance of the product and for future team projects:

1. Clearly assign responsibilities and split developer workload from the very start of a project, so every team member knows their scope of work upfront and overlap is minimized.

2. Address conflicts or disagreements as soon as they arise, instead of postponing them, to keep the team aligned throughout the Sprint.

We believe this project helped strengthen the team's soft skills, which should improve collaboration and communication in future projects.
